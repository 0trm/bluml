mlkb's update of 20 October 2026 folds 23 of Eugene Yan's machine learning posts into its notes, paraphrased and cited inline. The skills take those lessons through mlkb, and this page names the post behind each one, linked, not copied (© Eugene Yan). The numbers the skills use as defaults (the 1.5 to 3 multiplier for timeboxes, 2 to 4 weeks a year of upkeep, the 10% infrastructure budget, 1 to 5% output audits, a holdout of the newest seven days, seven past images kept) were checked against the posts themselves on 20 October 2026.

The MLflow material is checked against the [MLflow 3 documentation](https://mlflow.org/docs/latest/). The skills name MLflow only as a worked example. The skill format follows Anthropic's [skill authoring best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/agent-skills/best-practices), and the evals run on [`claude plugin eval`](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/plugin-evals).

- Huyen, systems design, [project setup](https://huyenchip.com/machine-learning-systems-design/design-a-machine-learning-system.html#project-setup-zlkQIG9): goals, how users meet the prediction, which error costs more.
- Crash Course [AutoML: benefits and limitations](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/crash-course/automl/benefits-limitations): a tool that cannot build even a mediocre model signals little predictive signal.
- The business case (step 5 and [reference/business-case.md](../skills/framing-models/reference/business-case.md)): mlkb 2.1 for estimating value by relating ML gains to business outcomes, and for the resources and timeline a project needs; Rules of ML #1 and #39 for keeping the heuristic when it does as well, #3 for the upkeep a complex heuristic carries too, and #41 for weighing a gain against the complexity it adds; Sculley et al. Yan, through mlkb 2.1, 4.1 and 4.2: net benefit for ranking candidate problems, extent against severity, and 2 to 4 weeks a year to operate and maintain a system ([*Choosing Problems in Data Science and Machine Learning*](https://eugeneyan.com/writing/how-to-choose-problems/)); timeboxes as stretch goals, multiplied by 1.5 to 3 for an estimate ([*Mechanisms for Effective Machine Learning Projects*](https://eugeneyan.com/writing/mechanisms-for-projects/)); a real-time infrastructure budget of 10% of the extra revenue, which the post gives as one example ([*Real-time Machine Learning for Recommendations*](https://eugeneyan.com/writing/real-time-recommendations/)); the cascade ([*More Design Patterns for Machine Learning Systems*](https://eugeneyan.com/writing/more-patterns/)) and the cache in front of a model ([*Design Patterns in Machine Learning Code and Systems*](https://eugeneyan.com/writing/design-patterns/)) as cheaper shapes.

### doubting-results

## Where the sources disagree

1. **The split.** The Crash Course shuffles, then partitions. Rules of ML #33, mlkb 3.2.4 and the interviews book (7.2) split by time. The skills split by time whenever rows are time-ordered; shuffling is acceptable only under the Crash Course's own conditions: independent, identically distributed rows from a stationary distribution. Yan's exception in mlkb 3.2.4, a link between inputs and label that holds over time (a product's category from its title), is that stationary case, and the skills ask for both splits to be run to show it.
2. **AUC.** The Crash Course compares models by AUC, and Rules of ML #9 gates export on it. The skills use AUC to compare candidates and as a sanity check, and judge a ship decision at the operating point the product uses.
3. **Overlap between training and test.** The Crash Course calls test sets with no overlapping keys artificially hard. A time split keeps entities on both sides when the product scores known entities again; the skills add an entity holdout when the product mostly scores new ones.
4. **Baseline.** The Crash Course's baseline is a first model that checks the pipeline. Huyen's random, human and heuristic baselines, mlkb's human-level performance and the lecture's rule are the bar to beat. `training-models` records both kinds and names which one a number is compared against.
5. **MLflow versions.** The notebooks use registry stages and `mlflow.evaluate`; the MLflow docs mark stages deprecated since 2.9 and `mlflow.evaluate` since 3.0. The video reads autologged metrics, which are computed on the training data. The skills use aliases, and a number computed on training data is never evidence.
6. **Ownership.** The interviews book places deployment and monitoring with MLOps, not ML engineering. The skills make the decisions; who runs the infrastructure is the team's call.
7. **Rollback or cause first.** Yan reverts a release that misbehaves and looks for the cause afterwards (mlkb 4.3.4); `monitoring-models` names the cause before acting. The skills roll back first when a move starts at a model release, where the rollback is cheap and a recovery is evidence, and find the cause first otherwise, since the old model reads a broken input too.
8. **Refitting after the release check.** Yan's check holds out the newest week, then retrains on all the data, holdout included, and releases that version, which was not itself scored (mlkb 4.4.3). The skills allow the refit for a fixed recipe and compare its predictions with the live model's before it ships.
9. **Upkeep.** Yan puts operating and maintaining a system at 2 to 4 weeks a year (mlkb 2.1.2); Sculley et al. find upkeep the larger cost over a system's life, and the worked cases budget a fifth or a quarter of an engineer. The skills take the team's own history first, and count monitors and reviewed retrains on top of Yan's figure.

## Left out

- **LLM applications:** the Crash Course LLM module, the video from 6:11 to 29:30 and notebooks 4 to 8, and the lecture's section on foundation models. What carries over: a fallback when a model or provider fails, versions that never change once recorded, an eval set before the prompt.
- **Interviews and careers:** most of the interviews book's part I and the booklet's practice exercises.
- **Math and computer science refreshers:** chapters 5 and 6 of the interviews book, except the base-rate and small-numbers questions.
- **ML platform work:** distributed training, feature stores built for other teams (including Yan's hierarchy of needs for them, mlkb 4.4.2), serving infrastructure (reverse proxies and mediators, mlkb 4.2.3), and raw data processed once for many consumers (mlkb 2.2.7).
- **Unsupervised models:** clustering and anomaly detection, where the label and split logic does not hold, including the anomaly detection Yan pairs with supervised models when labels are scarce and attackers adapt (mlkb 3.6.6).
- **Bandits as a design:** the skills keep the exploration slice and its sizes in practice, not the algorithms (epsilon-greedy, upper confidence bounds, Thompson sampling), their priors or their tuning (mlkb 4.5.8).
- **Synthetic data and curriculum learning:** useful where ground truth is scarce, as in security; the skills grow labels from real rows only (mlkb 3.8.2).
- **Team process:** pilots and copilots, ways to set timeboxes, end-to-end ownership (mlkb 2.1.6 and 1.2.4). The skills keep the methodology review, the multiplier from timebox to estimate, and overrides as a sign of distrust.
- **Data exploration basics:** summary statistics and plots before modelling (mlkb 2.1.4).
- **Not available:** the answers to the Crash Course quizzes and to the interviews book's questions are not published.
